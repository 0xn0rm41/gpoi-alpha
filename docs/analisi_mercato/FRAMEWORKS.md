## 1\. Overview of Frameworks

Market research and product development for GPOI Alpha will rely on three complementary operational tools.

The objective is to understand users' needs, collect real-world data, and use the resulting evidence to guide strategic decisions and product development.

| Framework | Analysis Area | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| JTBD (Jobs To Be Done) | User Needs Analysis | Identifies the problems users want to solve and the outcomes they want to achieve. |
| Surveys and Interviews | User Research | Collects qualitative and quantitative information about users' needs, behaviors, preferences, and expectations. |
| SWOT Analysis | Strategic Analysis | Identifies the product's strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats in relation to the market. |

## 2\. Jobs To Be Done (JTBD)

The Jobs To Be Done (JTBD) framework focuses on the reasons why users choose a product or service to achieve a specific outcome.

The fundamental principle is that users are not simply looking for features; they are looking for tools that help them solve real problems.

### Structure

A JTBD statement can follow this structure:

"When \[situation\], I want to \[motivation\], so I can \[desired outcome\]."

### Application to GPOI Alpha

Examples of potential Jobs To Be Done:

- When I miss a class, I want to easily access shared notes, so I can catch up with the lessons.
- When a new school year starts, I want to buy used textbooks from other students, so I can save money.
- When I have an idea to improve my school, I want to gather support from other students, so I can present it with greater credibility.
- When I need to organize a school activity, I want to easily inform other students, so I can increase participation.

These examples represent initial hypotheses that must be validated with users.

### Objectives

- Identify students' real problems.
- Understand the motivations behind their behaviors.
- Identify the solutions they currently use.
- Discover unmet needs.
- Identify opportunities for new product features.

### Output

A structured list of Jobs To Be Done, categorized by:

- Problem or need.
- Situation in which the need arises.
- Desired outcome.
- Currently used solution.
- Importance and frequency of the need.

## 3\. Surveys and Interviews

Surveys and interviews are direct research methods used to collect information from users and validate hypotheses formulated through JTBD.

The main difference concerns the type of information collected:

- **Interviews:** explore users' motivations, experiences, behaviors, and problems in depth.
- **Surveys:** collect structured responses from a larger number of participants, helping identify trends and patterns.

### 3.1 Qualitative Interviews

Interviews aim to develop an in-depth understanding of users' experiences.

Open-ended questions should be preferred, avoiding leading questions or presenting product features too early.

Example questions:

- How do students usually communicate with each other?
- What difficulties do you encounter when trying to access school-related information?
- How do you share class notes?
- How do you buy or sell used textbooks?
- Which tools do you currently use?
- What do you dislike about the solutions you currently use?
- Can you describe the last time you encountered this problem?

**Objectives:**

- Understand actual user behaviors.
- Identify recurring problems.
- Discover unmet or unexpressed needs.
- Identify the alternatives currently used by students.

### 3.2 Quantitative Surveys

Surveys make it possible to measure how widespread the problems identified during interviews actually are.

For GPOI Alpha, the following areas can be investigated:

| Area | Potential Information to Collect |
| --- | --- |
| Communication | Tools used, frequency, and difficulties. |
| Events | Interest in school activities and initiatives. |
| Ideas and Voting | Interest in participation and student representation. |
| Shared Notes | Frequency of sharing and difficulties accessing notes. |
| Used Textbooks | Buying, selling, and saving habits. |
| Adoption | Willingness to use a platform dedicated to their school. |

**Objectives:**

- Measure how widespread identified problems are.
- Identify the most requested features.
- Compare users' preferences.
- Validate hypotheses emerging from interviews.

### 3.3 Results Analysis

Research results must be organized and compared against the initial hypotheses.

It is important to distinguish between:

- Quantitative data: percentages, frequencies, and response distributions.
- Qualitative data: motivations, experiences, and observations.
- Evidence: findings actually collected.
- Assumptions: information that has not yet been validated.

**Output**

A research report containing:

- Number and characteristics of participants.
- Survey results.
- Main interview findings.
- Most frequently reported problems.
- Identified needs and feature priorities.
- Hypotheses that were confirmed, partially confirmed, or rejected.

Note: Research involving minors must comply with applicable privacy, data protection, and research participation requirements.

## 4\. SWOT Analysis

SWOT Analysis is a strategic tool used to evaluate a product or project across four dimensions.

### Structure

| Dimension | Meaning | Type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strengths | Internal advantages of the project. | Internal |
| Weaknesses | Internal limitations and disadvantages. | Internal |
| Opportunities | External market opportunities. | External |
| Threats | External market and environmental risks. | External |

### Application to GPOI Alpha

The following is an initial assessment that should be updated based on collected evidence.

**Strengths**

- Platform specifically designed for school communities.
- Integration of multiple features within a single environment.
- Ability to create separate communities for each school.
- Potential for direct student engagement.
- Open-source codebase.

**Weaknesses**

- Need to reach a critical mass of users within each school.
- Limited brand awareness.
- Need to establish user trust and consistent engagement.
- Limited resources for development and promotion.

**Opportunities**

- Digitalization of school communication.
- Increased student participation in school life.
- Growing interest in collaboration tools.
- Reuse and resale of school textbooks.
- Potential expansion into additional schools.

**Threats**

- Established use of free tools such as WhatsApp, Telegram, and other platforms.
- Existing school platforms already adopted by institutions.
- Potential difficulties obtaining school authorization.
- Organizational and regulatory obstacles.
- Challenges in maintaining active user participation.

### Objectives

- Evaluate the project's current position.
- Identify its main competitive advantages.
- Recognize weaknesses that need to be addressed.
- Identify market opportunities.
- Anticipate potential market risks.

### Output

A periodically updated SWOT matrix used to guide:

- Product development priorities.
- Product positioning.
- User acquisition strategies.
- Feature development.
- Expansion strategies.

## 5\. Integrated Operational Workflow

The three tools are used sequentially to transform collected information into concrete decisions.

| Phase | Framework | Objective | Output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1\. Understanding | JTBD | Identify users' problems and needs. | List of Jobs To Be Done. |
| 2\. Research | Interviews | Explore user experiences and behaviors. | Qualitative evidence. |
| 3\. Validation | Surveys | Measure how widespread identified needs are. | Quantitative data. |
| 4\. Synthesis | SWOT | Evaluate the product's strategic position. | SWOT matrix. |
| 5\. Decision-Making | Results Analysis | Define priorities and next steps. | Product development recommendations. |

### Methodological Principles

1. Initial hypotheses must not be treated as verified facts.
2. Never invent statistics or research findings.
3. Document the sources, dates, and sample sizes of all research.
4. Distinguish users' actual needs from their feature requests.
5. Prioritize actual user behaviors over stated intentions.
6. Regularly update the SWOT analysis based on new evidence.
7. Use research findings to guide product development decisions.

---

**Guiding Principle:** Understand users' needs through JTBD, validate them through surveys and interviews, and use the findings to guide strategic decisions through SWOT Analysis.
