## 1\. Introduction

Market research for GPOI Alpha focuses on understanding students' needs, identifying existing problems within school communities, and evaluating the product's potential value.

The research process is based on three complementary approaches:

- **JTBD:** Understand what students are trying to achieve and which problems they face.
- **Surveys and Interviews:** Collect qualitative and quantitative evidence directly from users.
- **SWOT Analysis:** Evaluate the project's internal capabilities and external market conditions.

The goal is not simply to identify desirable features, but to understand whether GPOI Alpha solves meaningful problems and creates value for school communities.

## 2\. Jobs To Be Done (JTBD)

Jobs To Be Done is a framework based on the idea that people do not simply buy products; they use them to accomplish specific goals or solve problems.

A job has three dimensions:

- **Functional:** What the user needs to accomplish.
- **Emotional:** How the user wants to feel.
- **Social:** How the user wants to be perceived by others.

### Application to GPOI Alpha

Instead of asking students which features they want in a school platform, JTBD investigates what they are trying to achieve in their everyday school life.

Examples:

| Situation | Desired Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Missing a lesson | Access shared notes and catch up with classmates. |
| Organizing a school event | Easily inform students and increase participation. |
| Having an idea for school improvement | Gather support and make student opinions visible. |
| Starting a new school year | Buy or sell used textbooks and save money. |
| Searching for school information | Find relevant information without navigating multiple communication channels. |

The objective is to understand the underlying needs rather than immediately translating every request into a product feature.

### Research Approach

JTBD insights should be collected primarily through interviews.

Questions should focus on real experiences:

- What were you trying to accomplish?
- What made it difficult?
- How did you solve the problem?
- Which tools did you use?
- What was missing from the existing solution?

**Expected Output:** A structured list of user needs, problems, motivations, and desired outcomes to guide product decisions.

## 3\. Surveys and Interviews

Surveys and interviews are research methods used to collect information directly from students and other relevant members of school communities.

They help validate JTBD hypotheses, understand user behavior, and measure how widespread identified problems are.

### 3.1 Interviews (Qualitative Research)

Interviews provide an in-depth understanding of individual experiences, motivations, and frustrations.

They should primarily use open-ended questions to encourage users to describe their actual behaviors.

Example questions:

- How do you currently communicate with classmates?
- How do you share and access school notes?
- How do you discover school events?
- How do you participate in school decisions?
- How do you buy or sell used textbooks?
- What difficulties do you encounter with the tools you currently use?

The focus should be on past experiences rather than hypothetical preferences.

### 3.2 Surveys (Quantitative Research)

Surveys help measure the frequency and relevance of problems identified during interviews.

For GPOI Alpha, research can focus on the following areas:

| Area | Research Objective |
| --- | --- |
| Communication | Understand how students exchange information. |
| Events | Measure interest in school activities and announcements. |
| Ideas and Voting | Evaluate student participation and representation. |
| Shared Notes | Understand how students access and share educational material. |
| Used Textbooks | Investigate buying, selling, and reuse habits. |
| Platform Adoption | Measure willingness to use a dedicated school community platform. |

Surveys can include multiple-choice questions, rating scales, and open-ended responses.

### 3.3 Research Analysis

Collected information should be organized into three categories:

- **Evidence:** Information directly supported by research.
- **Insights:** Patterns and conclusions identified from the evidence.
- **Assumptions:** Hypotheses that still require validation.

Results should help identify:

- Most common user problems.
- Most relevant needs.
- Existing solutions and their limitations.
- Features with the greatest potential value.
- Barriers to adopting GPOI Alpha.

**Expected Output:** A research report combining qualitative and quantitative findings, used to validate or reject initial assumptions.

## 4\. SWOT Analysis

SWOT Analysis is a strategic framework used to evaluate a project through four dimensions:

| Dimension | Description | Type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strengths | Internal advantages and capabilities. | Internal |
| Weaknesses | Internal limitations and areas for improvement. | Internal |
| Opportunities | External conditions that could benefit the project. | External |
| Threats | External factors that could negatively affect the project. | External |

### Application to GPOI Alpha

The SWOT analysis should be based on the findings collected through JTBD research, surveys, interviews, and project development.

**Strengths**

- Dedicated platform for school communities.
- Multiple student-oriented features in one environment.
- Separate communities for individual schools.
- Open-source codebase.
- Potential to encourage student collaboration and participation.

**Weaknesses**

- Requires sufficient participation within each school.
- Limited initial brand awareness.
- Dependence on student engagement.
- Limited development and marketing resources.

**Opportunities**

- Increasing digitalization of school communities.
- Need for better student communication and collaboration.
- Opportunities for student participation in school activities.
- Reuse and exchange of educational materials.
- Potential expansion to additional schools.

**Threats**

- Established communication tools such as WhatsApp and Telegram.
- Existing school management platforms.
- Organizational barriers to school adoption.
- Privacy and regulatory requirements.
- Difficulty maintaining long-term user engagement.

These points are initial hypotheses and must be updated as new evidence becomes available.

### Strategic Application

SWOT should not remain a descriptive exercise. Its findings should support practical decisions:

- Use strengths to exploit market opportunities.
- Address weaknesses that limit adoption.
- Identify external threats before they become critical.
- Prioritize product improvements based on validated user needs.

**Expected Output:** An evidence-based SWOT matrix that supports product prioritization and strategic planning.

---

## 5\. Research Principles

The following principles apply throughout the research process:

1. Prioritize real user problems over assumptions about desirable features.
2. Distinguish actual behaviors from hypothetical intentions.
3. Never present unverified assumptions as established facts.
4. Document research sources, dates, and participant samples.
5. Use qualitative research to understand problems and quantitative research to measure their relevance.
6. Update the SWOT analysis as new evidence emerges.
7. Consider students and schools as distinct stakeholders when evaluating adoption and value.
8. Ensure research involving minors complies with applicable privacy and data protection requirements.

---

**Guiding Principle:** Understand students' needs through JTBD, validate those needs through surveys and interviews, and translate the findings into strategic decisions through SWOT Analysis.
